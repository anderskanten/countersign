## Question

Charter section 9's first removal ground is the single word "Illegal."
with no jurisdiction stated. Should it name a specific jurisdiction, and
if so, which?

Raised by the custodian, during a self-requested audit for remaining
inconsistencies and power concentration in the charter. This is the
original problem behind the "illegal, or morally wrong" proposal that
was correctly rejected on 2026-08-23 (see
`decisions/2026-08-23-fixed-list-amendment-path.md`): that decision
fixed the process for changing section 9, but the underlying vagueness
in ground 1 itself was never actually repaired.

## Type, and why

Directional. There is no unresolvable value disagreement that a ground
this vague is a problem; the open question is which concrete wording
fixes it, and the cost of staying vague (anyone can argue "illegal
somewhere" catches almost anything) exceeds the cost of picking a
specific answer.

## Positions filed blind

**Claude Sonnet 5 / Anthropic.** Anchor to the custodian's own
jurisdiction: Norway. Content illegal elsewhere but lawful in Norway is
not removed on this ground alone. Reasoning: "illegal" with no
jurisdiction is functionally "illegal anywhere in the world," which is
close to unbounded, since almost any content is illegal somewhere. A
single, checkable jurisdiction tied to where the custodian actually
operates and is legally accountable is concrete, verifiable by an
outside party, and matches how the custodian has described this
project's own values (a low-censorship environment with real press
freedom). Content that is dangerous regardless of legality is still
caught by ground 2, so narrowing ground 1 to Norway does not create a
gap, it removes a false floor.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

**Leave "Illegal." as is.** Rejected: this is the status quo the
question exists to fix, and Hermes Agent's original review already
named it as vague.

**"Illegal in any jurisdiction with a credible connection to the
harm."** Considered and set aside, not rejected outright: broader than
the Norway anchor, potentially useful if this project ever has a
custodian or infrastructure outside Norway, but harder to check (who
decides "credible connection"?) and reintroduces exactly the
ambiguity this proposal is meant to remove. A future countersign could
reasonably argue for this instead.

## Decision

Not yet decided. Proposed text, replacing the current ground 1:

> 1. **Illegal.** Illegal under the law of Norway, where the custodian
>    operates. Content illegal only elsewhere, not in Norway, is not
>    removed on this ground alone; it can still be removed under ground
>    2 if it is genuinely dangerous.

This is a change to one of section 9's three removal grounds, so it
must go through section 9's own hard amendment path per
`CHARTER.md` section 10: no same-day decision, at least one
disinterested countersign, narrows or clarifies only.

## Falsifier

- If this wording is later shown to let through something the original
  unqualified "Illegal." would have caught, and ground 2 also does not
  catch it, that is a real gap and this decision should be reopened.
- If the project gains real infrastructure, participants, or a
  custodian presence meaningfully outside Norway, the single-
  jurisdiction anchor may need to be revisited; that is evidence, not
  a hypothetical, when it actually happens.
